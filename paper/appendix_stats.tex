\section{Statistical methods}
All inferential claims in this paper use one of four procedures, collected
here with their exact forms.

\subsection{Paired bootstrap over genes}
The head-to-head comparisons (Tables on the Azimuth protocol) pair
per-gene Spearman correlations $\rho_g^{(A)}, \rho_g^{(B)}$ across the
$G = 9$ V1 genes. With $d_g = \rho_g^{(A)} - \rho_g^{(B)}$, the statistic
is $\bar d = G^{-1}\sum_g d_g$ and the $p$-value for
$H_1: \bar d < 0$ is
\begin{equation}
p = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B} \mathbf{1}\!\left[
\frac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G} d_{\pi_b(g)} \le 0 \right],
\end{equation}
where $\pi_b$ is a uniform resample of $\{1,\dots,G\}$ with replacement and
$B = 10{,}000$. We bootstrap genes, not guides, because guides within a
gene share assay conditions and are not independent; gene-level resampling
matches the unit at which the models must generalize.

\subsection{Two-proportion $z$-test for flag-rate enrichment}
For the duplex-enrichment discovery, flag counts $x_1, x_2$ from $n_1, n_2$
independent pairs give $\hat p_i = x_i / n_i$ and
\begin{equation}
z = \frac{\hat p_1 - \hat p_2}
{\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)\left(n_1^{-1} + n_2^{-1}\right)}}, \qquad
\hat p = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{n_1 + n_2},
\end{equation}
valid here because $n_i \hat p_i$ and $n_i(1-\hat p_i)$ all exceed $10{,}000$.

\subsection{Spearman correlation and its standard error}
Per-gene correlations use the rank difference formula; standard errors, where
needed, use the Fisher $z$ transform
$z_g = \operatorname{arctanh}(\rho_g)$ with
$\mathrm{SE}(z_g) \approx 1/\sqrt{n_g - 3}$, and intervals are transformed
back with $\tanh$. We report means over genes without further weighting,
matching the published Azimuth protocol exactly so that comparisons against
published numbers are like-for-like.

\subsection{Multiplicity across break attempts}
We attempted six model variants against the Rule Set 2 replication bar.
Under a Bonferroni correction for six attempts, a break claim would require
$p < 0.0083$; no attempt approached significance in the favorable direction
(the best challenger trailed with $p = 0.9996$ for the leader), so the
multiplicity correction changes no conclusion. We state this because
repeated undisclosed attempts inflate break claims elsewhere in the
literature; our attempts, scripts, and seeds are all committed.

\subsection{Contamination control as a statistical procedure}
Train/test overlap was detected by exact 30-mer string matching between the
FC+RES and V1 files (1{,}836 overlapping sequences) and is prevented
structurally: the shared loader refuses \texttt{nodrug} rows in training
frames, and a hermetic test asserts zero overlap on every run. We treat
this as part of the statistical methodology, not an engineering detail,
because the overlap changed the headline conclusion (an apparent break at
$0.792$) and its removal reversed it.

\subsection{Seeds and determinism}
Every stochastic component (NumPy, PyTorch, scikit-learn) takes a fixed seed
recorded in its script; the bootstrap resamples use a separate generator
seeded at $0$. Runs are bitwise reproducible on the same library versions,
which are pinned in \texttt{requirements.txt} and echoed into every result
file's metadata.
