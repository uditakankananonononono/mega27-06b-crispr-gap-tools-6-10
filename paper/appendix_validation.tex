\section{Extended validation tables}
All numbers in this appendix are read directly from the committed results
files cited in each caption; nothing is re-estimated for the manuscript.

\subsection{Multiplex discovery statistics (Tool 6)}
From \texttt{results/realguide\_risk.json}: 4{,}692 real guides from
crisprSQL; strong self-fold rate $8.95\%$ (mean self-fold MFE $-1.87$
kcal/mol); GC-window violation rate $13.4\%$ ($9.0\%$ high-GC, $4.3\%$
low-GC). The named discovery --- same-gene duplex enrichment --- flags
$22.50\%$ of 30{,}000 same-gene guide pairs vs $15.87\%$ of 30{,}000
cross-gene pairs and $10.81\%$ of random-spacer controls (two-proportion
$z \approx 19.9$). Same-gene pairs share exonic sequence context, which
raises antiparallel complementarity between guides; the effect survives
the random-spacer control, so it is not a base-composition artifact.

\subsection{Cross-species transfer ladder (Tool 10)}
From \texttt{results/beat\_azimuth\_v1.json}--\texttt{v7.json}, mean
per-gene Spearman on V1 mouse (9 genes), train RES human:
ridge $0.268$ (v1), GNN $0.218$ (v2), GBT $0.480$ (v3), rank-ensemble
$0.474$ (v4), deep-stack tuned $0.370$ (v5, flawed), leak-free OOF stack
$0.490$ (v6), multi-assay RES+V2 $0.547$ (v7). Bar: same-split Rule Set 2
replication $0.569$. The bar stands; v6 is our best honest challenger.

\subsection{Off-target scorer replications: full per-study matrix}
\label{app:offtarget-matrix}
Table~\ref{tab:offtarget-matrix} gives the complete leave-one-study-out
per-study AUC matrix behind the off-target replication subsections of the
main text - the wave-1 (Hsu-style, CFD-style) and wave-2 (CCTop-style,
CROP-IT-style, CRISPRoff-style) replications, 16{,}472 guide--off-target
pairs across seven studies, all numbers machine-extracted from
\texttt{results/}\allowbreak\texttt{offtarget\_scorers.json}
and \texttt{results/}\allowbreak\texttt{offtarget\_scorers2.json}.
On mean AUC the published
ordering holds exactly: Hsu-style $0.556$ $<$ CCTop-style $0.565$ $<$
CRISPRoff-style $0.567$ $<$ CROP-IT-style $0.573$ $<$ CFD-style $0.582$,
and CFD-style beats Hsu-style in 5 of 7 studies, matching the primary
published comparison. The per-study matrix adds structure the means hide.
Per-study winners split across scorers: CRISPRoff-style takes Anderson
($0.784$), Cameron ($0.574$) and Kim16 ($0.715$); CFD-style takes Kim and
KimChromatin, the two chromatin-context studies; CROP-IT-style takes Cho;
and Hsu-style - the simplest scorer - takes Finkelstein, a near-saturated
positive set (6{,}743/6{,}792 positive) where every scorer sits at or
below $0.48$ and CRISPRoff-style collapses to $0.331$. No scorer wins a
majority of studies; the honest summary is that the published mean
ordering is real but the per-study best scorer is study-dependent, with
CFD-style never worse than second on the two chromatin studies.
